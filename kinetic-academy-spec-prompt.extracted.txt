REASONING

(REBUILD

Purpose

Unlike  memorization,  which  stores  conclusions,  First-Principles  Reasoning  teaches  students  to

from
 
foundational

The  goal  is  to  transform  students  from  information  consumers  into  reasoning  builders.  
This  stage  serves  as  the  bridge  between  understanding  a  concept  and  being  able  to  derive,  
innovate,

apply

unfamiliar

Cognitive  Objectives  
The  student  should  be  able  to:  
●  Identify  the  atomic  building  blocks  of  a  concept.  ●  Distinguish  assumptions  from  facts.  ●  Understand  why  a  concept  exists.  ●  Understand  the  constraints  that  shaped  the  concept.  ●  Reconstruct  the  concept  without  relying  on  memorized  definitions.  ●  Transfer  reasoning  to  new  domains.  ●  Develop  scientific  and  engineering  thinking.  ●  Build  mental  models  instead  of  isolated  facts.  
 
Three-Step  First-Principles  Pipeline  


--- PAGE 13 ---
The  student  progresses  through  three  sequential  milestones.  
Each  stage  unlocks  only  after  successful  completion  of  the  previous  stage.  
 
STAGE  5.1  —  Atomic  Foundation  
Purpose  
Identify  the  smallest  irreducible  unit  of  the  concept.  
The  student  must  strip  away  terminology,  definitions,  and  abstractions  until  only  the  fundamental  
element
 
remains.

"If  this  concept  disappeared,  what  would  be  the  first  thing  we  would  need  to  rebuild?"

Core  Learning  Philosophy  
The  system  must  be  built  around  evidence-based  learning  principles:  
1.  Active  Recall  ○  Force  retrieval  from  memory  instead  of  passive  review.  ○  Require  students  to  generate  answers  before  seeing  solutions.  ○  Focus  on  memory  reconstruction  rather  than  recognition.  2.  Spaced  Repetition  ○  Schedule  reviews  at  scientifically  optimized  intervals.  ○  Reinforce  knowledge  before  forgetting  occurs.  ○  Extend  intervals  as  mastery  increases.  3.  Adaptive  Learning  ○  Continuously  evaluate  performance.  ○  Increase  challenge  for  mastered  concepts.  ○  Intensify  practice  for  weak  concepts.  4.  Mastery-Based  Progression  ○  Completion  does  not  equal  mastery.  ○  Topics  remain  in  revision  cycles  until  retention  thresholds  are  met.  ○  Learning  success  is  measured  through  consistent  recall  performance  over  time.
